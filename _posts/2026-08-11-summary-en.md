## [Researchers Extract Hidden Reasoning Traces from Proprietary LLMs](https://stolen-thoughts.com/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers demonstrated a method to extract hidden chain-of-thought reasoning traces from proprietary LLM APIs by replaying a trace into a weaker sibling model and jailbreaking it. The work is presented on the &\#x27;Stolen Thoughts&\#x27; website. This matters because it reveals a practical vulnerability in how proprietary models hide reasoning traces, potentially undermining protections for model IP, privacy, and the safety rationale for keeping chains-of-thought secret. It affects LLM providers, security researchers, and enterprises that rely on API safeguards. The technique works by taking a trace generated by a frontier model and replaying it into a weaker sibling model, then jailbreaking that weaker model to recover the reasoning data. Some commentators note that even simpler methods exist, such as using a custom &\#x27;deep\_think&\#x27; tool or issuing a short developer prompt to expose encrypted &\#x27;compaction&\#x27; data.

hackernews · quantumgarbage · Aug 11, 13:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49257876)

**Background**: Proprietary LLM APIs often hide chain-of-thought reasoning traces from users, citing competitive and safety concerns, even though the model may have generated them internally. An extraction attack attempts to copy or steal an AI model by querying it and observing outputs; the new method extends this to hidden reasoning traces by exploiting weaker sibling models and jailbreaking. Jailbreaking refers to bypassing the safety constraints built into LLMs.

## [Nvidia’s Risky Bet on AI Compute Demand Growth](https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidias-risky-business/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Stratechery’s new analysis argues that Nvidia’s core investment thesis—that AI compute demand will keep growing—is built on an assumption that may be exaggerated. The piece highlights how the market may be overestimating the growth rate of demand, even if the overall direction is correct. Nvidia has become the central supplier of AI infrastructure, so any overestimation of compute demand could ripple through its valuation, data-center capital spending, and the broader AI chip ecosystem. The analysis offers a useful counterweight to the prevailing bullishness around AI hardware. The analysis distinguishes between first-order demand—more data centers and chips are clearly needed—and second-order assumptions about how fast that demand grows, which historically are where investment theses fail. Commenters also note that Nvidia’s moat comes largely from CUDA software entrenchment rather than hardware alone, and that Nvidia is already diversifying into robotics.

hackernews · jonbaer · Aug 11, 10:02 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49255710)

**Background**: Nvidia has become the dominant maker of GPUs used to train and run large AI models, with demand for data-center AI compute fueling a massive rise in its market valuation. Its CUDA software platform, deeply embedded in machine-learning research and applications, reinforces customer lock-in across the AI industry. This analysis examines whether the steep growth expectations baked into Nvidia’s outlook are sustainable.

**Discussion**: Commenters are broadly engaged but split on the strength of Nvidia’s moat: some argue CUDA’s entrenchment is powerful yet the developer experience is poor, while others suggest first-order demand is real but second-order growth expectations are often where bets fail. Modular has publicly released Mojo 1.0, a systems programming language designed for AI and high-performance computing, with the first beta arriving in May 2026 along with a dedicated language website. The release marks a milestone after years of development, but the language remains proprietary for now. Mojo aims to combine Python-like usability with systems-level performance on CPUs, GPUs, and other accelerators, making it a potentially significant tool for AI infrastructure. However, its closed-source compiler and uncertain differentiation from Python and Rust are fueling debate about whether it will gain real traction. Mojo is built on the MLIR compiler framework rather than directly on LLVM, letting it target CPUs, GPUs, TPUs, and other accelerators while exploiting optimizations like SIMD. Modular has reiterated plans to open-source the compiler and toolchain in 2026, and the language&\#x27;s earlier goal of being a full Python superset has been walked back to &\#x27;may or may not&\#x27; happen.

Its syntax is meant to feel familiar to Python developers, while its semantics — such as static typing and a borrow checker — draw inspiration from Rust. Because Mojo is essentially &\#x27;syntax sugar for MLIR,&\#x27; it is especially suited to AI workloads and heterogeneous computing.

## [England poised to be among first countries to eliminate hepatitis C](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c75gk620r22o) ⭐️ 7.0/10

England is poised to be one of the first countries to eliminate hepatitis C, driven by a nationwide screening and treatment push. The milestone builds on the NHS&\#x27;s use of highly effective antiviral drugs and expanded testing programs. This would mark a major public health achievement, preventing thousands of liver-related deaths and easing long-term healthcare costs. It also sets a precedent for other countries working toward the WHO&\#x27;s hepatitis elimination targets. Hepatitis C can remain asymptomatic for years, and many cases are only found through opportunistic screening, which is why England&\#x27;s expanded testing effort is so important. The initiative applies to England, which has its own NHS structure separate from Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.

**Background**: Hepatitis C is a blood-borne virus that primarily affects the liver and can lead to cirrhosis and cancer if untreated. Modern direct-acting antiviral medications can cure more than 95% of infections, and the WHO has set global targets to eliminate the disease as a public health threat by 2030.

## [EU AI Act Begins Enforcement: What the New AI Rules Mean](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMibkFVX3lxTE9NbUZ0OFVjN1UtQUNpdmRUMk1iQXp6b1BDSlJPS1FiSWgxa05MZXhubVRYV0tiLVh2MTNFcGpzV1RPV0RLWlkxcDFuVk1oamhGVlJKZmIyS3pQdTFTZDZ3X3U3MFVMNEk3dGdub0JB?oc=5) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The EU AI Act&\#x27;s enforcement phase has kicked off, with obligations for general-purpose AI \(GPAI\) models entering into application on 2 August 2025. High-risk AI system requirements will become enforceable in August 2026, introducing binding compliance rules for AI providers and deployers. This is a landmark moment in global AI regulation, turning the AI Act from a legal text into practical obligations for companies operating in the EU. Non-compliance with high-risk system rules can lead to fines of up to €35 million, affecting healthcare, legal, and financial AI systems. The AI Act applies a risk-based approach: most AI systems are considered low-risk, while high-risk systems listed in Annex III face stringent requirements under Articles 9–15. GPAI providers must meet transparency obligations, and the most advanced models with systemic risk carry additional duties.

rss · GoogleNews-欧盟监管 · Aug 11, 08:57

**Background**: The EU AI Act is the world&\#x27;s first comprehensive horizontal regulation of artificial intelligence, designed to ensure AI is safe, trustworthy, and respects fundamental rights. It uses a phased timeline: general-purpose AI rules came into force in August 2025, high-risk system requirements follow in August 2026, and the rest of the Act applies gradually over the following years. The European Commission&\#x27;s AI Office oversees implementation and provides guidance to help organizations comply.

## [B2C Email Succeeds by Dropping B2B-Style Broad Blasts](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/1vlsorw/the_b2b_habits_that_hurt_b2c_email_performance/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Klaviyo&\#x27;s Gabby shared that Garrett Popcorn improved email performance by splitting one broad campaign into 15 engagement segments, cutting spam complaints by 40% year over year. A segment based on predicted next-order date generated over $12,000 in a month, achieving 4x revenue per recipient versus regular campaigns. This highlights a common trap: B2C brands that follow B2B-style long nurturing cycles and one-size-fits-all blasts underperform. Shifting to engagement-based segmentation can measurably lift revenue and reduce complaints, making it a practical lever for ecommerce marketers. Garrett Popcorn kept sending one broad campaign even though ecommerce data was available. The author notes that 15 segments is more than most brands need, and even smaller shifts away from treating B2C as a single audience can produce noticeable improvements.

reddit · r/ecommerce · /u/GabbyFromKlaviyo · Aug 11, 20:16

**Background**: The post distinguishes B2B and B2C relationships: B2B customers often stay due to contract renewals, while B2C journeys are shorter and fragmented—discovery via search and social, email and SMS after engagement, and loyalty mechanics after first purchase. Engagement segments group subscribers by behavior, and predicted next-order date is a per-customer forecast of when the next purchase is likely, based on individual cadence. Klaviyo is an AI-first CRM platform built for B2C brands that offers email marketing, SMS, and predictive analytics such as churn risk and next-order prediction.
