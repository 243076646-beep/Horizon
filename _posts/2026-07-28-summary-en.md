The EU AI Act has officially become law, imposing a transparency deadline within six days and banning nudifier apps by December 2024. This marks a significant regulatory milestone for AI in the EU, setting binding rules that affect developers and deployers, including bans on high-risk applications like nudifier apps. The transparency deadline likely applies to general-purpose AI systems requiring disclosure of training data and capabilities. The ban on nudifier apps targets AI tools that remove clothing from images without consent.

**Background**: The EU AI Act, proposed in April 2021 and passed in 2024, classifies AI systems by risk levels \(unacceptable, high, limited, minimal\). Unacceptable risk systems are banned outright. Nudifier apps fall under unacceptable risk as they can be used for non-consensual intimate image abuse. The Act has phased implementation, with some provisions becoming effective soon after entry into force.

The European Union released a transparency guide for AI systems, outlining compliance requirements ahead of the August enforcement deadline for the EU AI Act. This guide is critical for organizations deploying AI in the EU, as non-compliance could result in significant fines. It clarifies transparency obligations, helping businesses prepare for the landmark regulation. The guide covers transparency requirements for high-risk AI systems, including documentation, user information, and risk management.
