## [Go 1.27 Adds Generic Methods, Post-Quantum Crypto, Standard UUID](https://go.dev/blog/go1.27) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Go 1.27 has been released with major new capabilities, including support for generic methods, new post-quantum cryptography packages such as crypto/mldsa, and a new standard library package for UUID generation. It also introduces a new floating-point parsing and formatting algorithm called uscale. This release is significant because generic methods have been a highly requested feature ever since generics arrived in Go 1.18, and adding post-quantum crypto to the standard library helps the ecosystem prepare for future quantum-computing threats. Developers using the stdlib UUID package may also reduce dependency on third-party libraries such as google/uuid. Generic methods allow methods to declare their own type parameters, but the proposal does not cover generic interface methods. The new post-quantum package crypto/mldsa implements the ML-DSA digital signature scheme; floating-point parsing and formatting in the standard library now use Russ Cox&\#x27;s uscale algorithm.

hackernews · database64128 · Aug 19, 18:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49365405)

**Background**: Go is a statically typed, compiled programming language created at Google, known for its simplicity and strong standard library. Generics were added in Go 1.18, letting functions and types be parameterized, but methods could not declare type parameters until now. Post-quantum cryptography \(PQC\) refers to algorithms designed to be secure against quantum computers; NIST released its first three PQC standards in 2024. The Go team has been actively adding PQC packages to prepare for the eventual transition.

## [Ecommerce Merchants Face New Challenge: Verifying Human-Backed AI Agents at Checkout](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/1vson9r/how_do_i_tell_if_an_ai_agent_hitting_my_checkout/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An ecommerce operator posted on Reddit asking how to distinguish legitimate AI shopping agents from bots that abuse sales, discount codes, and limited stock. The post highlights early tools AgentKit and Skyfire, which aim to provide &\#x27;human-backed&\#x27; proof for AI agents. As AI agents increasingly act as economic actors at checkout, merchants need a way to know whether a real, accountable person stands behind each purchase. This discussion signals a growing demand for &\#x27;agent KYC&\#x27; and proof-of-human infrastructure in ecommerce and beyond. AgentKit, built on World ID and developed with Coinbase, provides programmable proof that a unique human is behind an agent, while Skyfire offers agents verified identity and payment credentials that work across the open internet. The original poster notes the space is still early, with no agreed-upon standard or easily pluggable integration for existing store setups.

reddit · r/ecommerce · /u/nullpointerr404 · Aug 19, 14:51

**Background**: AI shopping agents are software programs that browse and buy products on behalf of a person, which raises new fraud and verification challenges for merchants. Traditional KYC \(Know Your Customer\) verifies the identity of a human customer, and &\#x27;agent KYC&\#x27; extends this idea to verify that an AI agent is tied to an accountable person. World ID is a proof-of-human protocol, and AgentKit applies it to the agentic web; Skyfire is an &\#x27;agent trust stack&\#x27; that gives agents authenticated access and payment credentials. These tools aim to let merchants get a yes/no answer about accountability without seeing full identity details.

A study highlighted in Tech Times found that deleting the policy text from an EU AI Act guard model&\#x27;s input leaves its verdicts unchanged, meaning the model evaluates content based on learned patterns rather than actually reading the rules. This challenges the assumption that AI safety judges ground their decisions in explicit policy, which has direct implications for EU AI Act compliance and AI safety evaluation. If guardrails ignore the rules they are supposed to enforce, regulators and developers may overestimate their reliability. The Tech Times article notes that a guard model trained on financial compliance violations learns to recognize scenarios that look like violations, making the rule text in its input &\#x27;decorative&\#x27; for practical purposes. An arXiv paper \(2605.06161\) reports that content-preserving policy rewrites flip up to 9.1% of verdicts above baseline jitter and introduces a Policy Invariance Score to expose such judge reliability issues.

rss · GoogleNews-欧盟监管 · Aug 19, 13:51

**Background**: The EU AI Act is a landmark regulation that sets requirements for AI systems, including general-purpose AI models. Guard models, or AI guardrails, are safety components designed to filter or classify harmful content in AI systems. Many developers rely on LLM-as-a-judge pipelines, where an AI model evaluates another model&\#x27;s outputs against a policy rubric. This study shows such judges may not genuinely read the rubric, raising concerns about current safety evaluation practices.
