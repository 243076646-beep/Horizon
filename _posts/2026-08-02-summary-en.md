## [How Google Helped Destroy RSS Feed Adoption](https://openrss.org/blog/how-google-helped-destroy-adoption-of-rss-feeds) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An article on openrss.org argues that Google, especially by shutting down Google Reader in 2013, significantly contributed to RSS&\#x27;s decline and the rise of walled gardens. It frames this as a pivotal moment in the open web&\#x27;s history. RSS is an open standard that lets users aggregate content from many sources, and its decline helped centralize content into a few closed platforms. This matters for anyone concerned about web openness, user control, and the shift toward ad-driven walled gardens. The article points to Google Reader&\#x27;s shutdown and the company&\#x27;s push of Google+ as key factors, with Google&\#x27;s &\#x27;declining usage&\#x27; excuse widely seen as disingenuous. Firefox also removed RSS subscription features in Firefox 64, further eroding RSS adoption.

hackernews · pudgywalsh · Aug 1, 18:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49136821)

**Background**: RSS \(Really Simple Syndication\) is an XML-based format that lets websites publish updates that users can subscribe to and read in a feed reader, avoiding the need to visit each site. Google Reader was launched in 2005 and became the most popular RSS reader, but Google shut it down in 2013, citing declining usage. A walled garden, in contrast, is a closed ecosystem where a single company controls access to content and services, such as social media platforms and app stores.

## [EU AI Act Main Phase Begins August 2, 2026](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMidkFVX3lxTE9vVi1fWlRRSHpFNjZGYk5ERWZlUkFuaWxxdjh6WEJ6U0llZjNmQ3VaeHZtS2h1TjBXbVNSbWh5THEwRG5hNzhISVRBYWZkVTNFSnkyZGwza0lldWxlZ1RfU3BXRUZ5VDBNYkgzZTdDMWZWS19vcFE?oc=5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act enters its main implementation phase, making broad AI obligations and transparency requirements enforceable across the European Union. The European Commission has simultaneously launched an AI enforcement team to oversee compliance and impose fines. This milestone makes the EU the first jurisdiction to enforce a comprehensive AI law, affecting any company that provides or uses AI systems in the EU market. It creates a global compliance benchmark and raises the stakes for AI developers and deployers. The AI Act applies a risk-based framework, with most general-purpose AI and transparency rules taking effect now; high-risk system obligations follow by August 2027. Fines for prohibited practices can reach €35 million or 7% of a company&\#x27;s global annual turnover, whichever is higher.

**Background**: The EU AI Act is the world&\#x27;s first comprehensive regulation of artificial intelligence, published in the EU Official Journal in July 2024. It entered into force on 1 August 2024, followed by phased deadlines: prohibitions from February 2025, general-purpose AI obligations from August 2025, and the main obligations from August 2026. The Act classifies AI systems by risk level—unacceptable, high, limited, and minimal—to determine the required compliance measures.
