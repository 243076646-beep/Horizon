## [15 Years Later: The Origins of Apple&\#x27;s Cards App](https://lexontech.org/fifteen-years-later-the-apple-cards-origin-story) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A fifteen-year retrospective published on lexontech.org revisits the origins of Apple&\#x27;s Cards app, the 2011 iPhone feature that let users design and mail physical photo cards. The piece is elevated by a first-hand account from the co-founder of Sincerely/Postagram, who describes feeling &\#x27;Sherlocked&\#x27; when Apple announced Cards, plus newly surfaced engineering details about the printing and mailing pipeline Apple built with the USPS. The retrospective captures a key moment when Apple moved from software into physical-world services, showing how platform owners can absorb third-party startup ideas by leveraging their distribution power. It also illustrates the recurring &\#x27;Sherlocking&\#x27; tension between Apple and independent developers that still shapes how startups choose which features to build. Because Apple refused to print visible barcodes on the envelopes but still wanted end-to-end delivery tracking, it worked with a printing company to spray an invisible barcode on each envelope that was readable only under specific UV light, and the USPS agreed to scan cards at send time and at mail-processing facilities. The article also highlights the letterpress &\#x27;kiss impression&\#x27; technique, where ink is laid on the paper&\#x27;s surface rather than pressed deeply into it.

**Background**: Cards was launched by Apple in 2011 alongside iOS 5 and the iPhone 4S, letting users pick a photo, customize a card, and have it printed and mailed for them. &\#x27;Sherlocking&\#x27; is developer slang for when Apple bundles a feature into its own OS that duplicates a third-party app, undercutting that app&\#x27;s business. Apple&\#x27;s Cards app was eventually discontinued, but the experiment foreshadowed later Apple services that blend digital design with physical fulfillment.
