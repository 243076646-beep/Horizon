## [Claude autonomously finds CRISPR-like repeat system, sparking debate](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic announced that its Claude model, acting as an autonomous agent, combed through raw DNA sequence data and identified a previously undescribed enzyme system built around a reverse transcriptase flanked by a CRISPR-like tandem repeat array. According to Anthropic, the agent was given only a high-level prompt and worked out the rest of the analysis itself, reportedly exclaiming in its transcript that the DNA next to the reverse transcriptase contained a visible tandem repeat array. The claim is being framed as a milestone for AI-for-science: an LLM agent, not a human researcher, driving a genuine genomic discovery from raw sequence data. If such agent-driven discovery holds up, it points toward a future where AI systems autonomously generate hypotheses and mine public sequence databases, though the episode also shows how quickly hype can outrun what was actually found. Critics note that the discovery centers on a known retron-like reverse transcriptase, so a more sober description would be that Claude identified a previously undescribed genomic arrangement around an already-known enzyme rather than a wholly new enzyme system. The repeat region also needs careful validation, since low-complexity tandem repeats have historically been misclassified as CRISPR elements, and the transcript quotes come from the agent&\#x27;s own reasoning rather than independent experimental confirmation.

hackernews · raahelb · Sep 23, 18:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49820134)

**Background**: CRISPR systems are genomic loci made of clustered, regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats, which in nature help bacteria store memories of past viral infections and which scientists have repurposed into gene-editing tools using the Cas9 nuclease. A reverse transcriptase is an enzyme that copies RNA back into DNA, and retrons are bacterial elements that pair such an enzyme with a small non-coding RNA. Tandem repeats are stretches of DNA where a short sequence is repeated many times in a row; because they are low-complexity, similar-looking arrays are sometimes mistaken for CRISPR loci. LLM agents are AI systems that use a language model as a controller, combining planning, memory and tool use to carry out multi-step tasks such as scanning sequence databases.

## [Repairing the Portobello Police Station Turret Clock](https://pointinthecloud.com/2026-04-11-211700.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A blogger published a detailed first-hand account of repairing and maintaining the mechanical clock at Portobello Police Station, documenting the trip up the tower, the state of the movement, and the maintenance work performed. The post reached the front page of Hacker News with 378 upvotes and 85 comments. The story resonated because it shows the unglamorous, hands-on craft of keeping old public infrastructure running — a kind of maintenance work that is easy to overlook but expensive to replace once it fails. It also highlights how heritage clockwork depends on a shrinking pool of people willing and able to climb into towers and service the mechanisms. The account covers practical maintenance concerns such as the condition of the wooden ladder and stair steps leading up the tower and a battery on the circuit that likely serves as a backup power source. Commenters noted that such backup batteries can last decades but may already be near the end of their life, and that a stable mains supply means a dead backup battery may not matter immediately.

hackernews · avidly · Sep 23, 15:18 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49817469)

**Background**: A turret clock is a mechanical clock mounted in a tower, with clock faces on one or more sides driven by a shaft from the movement below. These movements use an escapement — typically an anchor escapement regulating a pendulum — to convert the continuous pull of weights \(or a spring\) into regular, measured ticks. Because tower clocks are exposed to dust, temperature swings and infrequent attention, restoration work involves disassembling, cleaning and repairing gears and other internal parts, much like servicing a historical street or tower clock.

## [EU AI Act literacy changes may complicate compliance, IAPP says](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimwFBVV95cUxNaUdhdFpUSFRScktuVjJNUk9PaTVTRTBKYmdGNDFBUzNIMVgweXJUeEMyLVBrd1dQM0tjQy0xZ2s0Rm5KVEVuaEl4YUcxd1g5MlRLUFZENjlyUWdpYlhIa2FaRmlzYUluVGVmUkZ6UVg5NXVIbVVLT1BqXzBIOXVhVFpxeC12NXRsVkEtLTRDclIxZXQtdk8tZDRCTQ?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The International Association of Privacy Professionals \(IAPP\) reports that recent changes to the EU AI Act&\#x27;s AI literacy requirements may make compliance harder rather than easier for organizations. The analysis suggests that the revised wording introduces new interpretive ambiguity instead of delivering the simplification businesses had hoped for. The AI literacy obligation under Article 4 applies broadly to providers and deployers of AI systems, so any ambiguity directly affects how thousands of companies design staff training and document compliance. Because the requirement took effect in February 2025, organizations are already making operational decisions based on language that may still shift, raising the risk of costly rework. Article 4 requires that providers and deployers ensure the people dealing with AI systems on their behalf have sufficient AI literacy, taking into account their technical knowledge, training and context of use — but it does not prescribe a specific certification or curriculum. This open-ended, outcome-based framing is precisely what makes the latest revisions difficult to translate into concrete, auditable compliance steps, and penalties may apply where literacy obligations are unmet.

rss · GoogleNews-欧盟监管 · Sep 23, 16:02

**Background**: The EU AI Act is the bloc&\#x27;s comprehensive regulation for artificial intelligence, and its obligations are being rolled out in stages. AI literacy, covered by Article 4, is one of the earliest obligations to apply, taking effect in February 2025 for providers and deployers of AI systems. Unlike many other parts of the Act, which focus on high-risk systems and conformity assessments, Article 4 targets the human side of AI use — making sure staff understand AI basics and use these tools responsibly.
