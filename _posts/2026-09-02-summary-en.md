## [Small Transformer Trained in 1.5 Hours Beats Many LLMs on ARC](https://mvakde.github.io/blog/44-on-arc-1/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A developer trained a small autoregressive transformer from scratch on ARC puzzles in just 1.5 hours, and it achieved results competitive with much larger LLMs. The project explicitly shows that complex reasoning benchmarks can be tackled without relying on massive language models. This is significant because it challenges the common assumption that state-of-the-art reasoning requires enormous model scale and training budgets. It points toward sample-efficient, architecturally focused approaches that could lower the cost of AI reasoning research and broaden who can participate. The author clarified that this is not an LLM but a small autoregressive transformer, and that ARC is a meta-learning benchmark where learning from evaluation puzzles is legitimate; the labels were never used. Score improvements came mainly from modern architecture choices such as SwiGLU and RMSNorm, better data diversity and shuffling, and scaling from 4 to 8 layers.

hackernews · porridgeraisin · Sep 1, 09:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49519939)

**Background**: ARC-AGI-1, the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus, was introduced by François Chollet in 2019 as a benchmark for measuring AI systems&\#x27; ability to handle novel reasoning problems they were not prepared for. Sample efficiency refers to how little data a system needs to reach proficiency; humans learn from relatively few examples, while models often require orders of magnitude more data. This project highlights an alternative direction where careful architecture and training choices reduce data and compute requirements on a challenging reasoning benchmark.

A Newsonaut opinion piece argues that despite Mozilla&\#x27;s missteps, Firefox remains essential for browser engine diversity and competition. The article&\#x27;s comment section on Hacker News turned into a substantive debate about ad-blocking, Mozilla&\#x27;s trade-offs, and the danger of a browser monoculture. Firefox is the only major non-Chrome and non-WebKit browser, so its survival directly determines whether the web remains a multi-engine ecosystem. If Firefox fades, web developers and users lose a vital counterweight to Chromium&\#x27;s dominance, which could weaken web standards and reduce user choice. Commentators note that Chrome forks do not count as independent browsers because they share the same engine and cannot easily diverge from upstream. Others highlight that Firefox offers access to high-quality ad blockers, and one user shared an alternative WPT test-results viewer showing the progress of alternative engines like Servo and Ladybird.

hackernews · speckx · Sep 1, 20:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49527748)

**Background**: Most major browsers today are built on just a few engines: Chromium&\#x27;s Blink \(Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera\) and WebKit \(Safari\). Firefox uses its own Gecko engine, making it one of the last meaningful alternatives for keeping the web open and interoperable. Browser engine diversity matters because standards are tested by real implementations, and a single dominant engine can subtly dictate what web developers can and cannot do. The Web Platform Tests \(WPT\) suite measures how well each engine conforms to those standards.

**Discussion**: The comments are largely supportive of the article but openly critical of Mozilla, with users citing acquisitions, data collection, and &\#x27;anti-features&\#x27; that push users away. Several commenters nevertheless agree that Firefox is the last best hope for engine diversity, while one defends it as the only browser with quality ad blockers. There is also debate about developers&\#x27; responsibility for the web&\#x27;s current state, since testing mostly on Chromium reinforces the monoculture.

## [Mozilla Introduces Built-in Ad Blocker for Firefox on iOS](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/firefox/ad-blocker-on-ios/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Mozilla has begun rolling out an experimental built-in ad blocker for Firefox on iOS, though it is not yet generally available. The feature is being gradually enabled for a subset of users as part of a controlled experiment. This is a notable step toward integrating privacy features directly into Firefox on Apple&\#x27;s platform, where all browsers must use WebKit. Because the rollout is limited and requires telemetry, its immediate impact is modest, and users may continue to rely on third-party blockers or alternative browsers. The ad blocker uses WebKit&\#x27;s native content blocking API, as Firefox for iOS is built on WebKit. It blocks ads on web pages but does not filter ads shown on search engine results pages, a limitation that has drawn criticism from users.

hackernews · HieronymusBosch · Sep 1, 13:46 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49521973)

**Background**: iOS restricts all third-party browsers to Apple&\#x27;s WebKit engine and supports content blockers through a native extension API that applies JSON-based rules to hide or block page elements. Firefox for iOS therefore relies on WebKit, and unlike desktop Firefox where ad-blocking extensions are common, the iOS version must build the functionality directly into the browser using the WebKit content blocker API. The experimental feature adds an integrated toggle in Firefox settings rather than requiring a separate blocker app.

## [Ninth Circuit Ruling on Perplexity vs Amazon Could Reshape AI Ecommerce](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/1w436w4/the_ninth_circuits_perplexity_vs_amazon_ruling/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Ninth Circuit overturned an injunction that had blocked Perplexity&\#x27;s Comet browser from shopping on Amazon, ruling that when a user instructs the agent, the user—not Perplexity—accesses Amazon&\#x27;s servers. This weakens Computer Fraud and Abuse Act claims against AI shopping agents. The decision makes it significantly harder for Amazon and other platforms to block AI shopping agents through computer-fraud lawsuits, shifting the battleground to technical protocols and trust mechanisms. Ecommerce sellers will need to adapt to a world where agents mediate purchases and declared product data must be verifiable. The court reasoned that Comet acts only on user instruction, so the legal access is by the user, not Perplexity. The author highlights three emerging approaches—OpenAI/Stripe&\#x27;s scoped single-use payment token, Google&\#x27;s AP2 mandate-based credentials, and Shopify&\#x27;s UCP common catalogue language—and notes that the unsolved trust problem is whether sellers&\#x27; claims about inventory, fulfillment, or delivery can be independently verified.

reddit · r/ecommerce · /u/PuzzledBag931 · Sep 1, 05:58

**Background**: The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act \(CFAA\) prohibits unauthorized access to protected computers, and Amazon and other platforms have argued that AI agents violate it by accessing their sites. In Van Buren v. United States \(2021\), the Supreme Court narrowed what counts as &\#x27;exceeds authorized access,&\#x27; giving agents a stronger argument that when a user authorizes an agent, the access is authorized. Meanwhile, protocols like OpenAI&\#x27;s delegated payment spec and Stripe&\#x27;s shared one-time tokens are emerging to let agents transact without exposing card details.

## [Using Public Customs Records to Vet Suppliers Saves Time and Money](https://www.reddit.com/r/ecommerce/comments/1w4csvt/i_wasted_most_of_my_first_year_sourcing_because_i/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A Reddit user shares that they wasted most of their first year sourcing through directories and cold emails, and even lost a deposit to a trading company posing as a factory, before discovering that many countries publish customs records. These records let them see who actually shipped what to whom, transforming their supplier vetting from a trust-based Q&amp;A into an evidence-based comparison. This tip is significant for importers and ecommerce sellers because it offers a concrete, publicly accessible way to verify suppliers before paying deposits, potentially saving money and reducing fraud risk. It makes sourcing more efficient and objective, and it is a widely applicable strategy across many countries that publish customs data. Public customs data is available from many sources, including the U.S. CBP Public Data Portal and commercial platforms such as Port Examiner and ImportGenius. The records typically show the shipper, receiver, cargo description, and shipment frequency, which can be compared against what a supplier claims during a call.

reddit · r/ecommerce · /u/Silly\_Captain2089 · Sep 1, 13:59

**Background**: Customs records are generated every time a container crosses a border, and in many countries that paperwork is accessible to the public. This data includes bill of lading details, which list the parties, goods, and shipping route, giving importers a multi-year view of a company&\#x27;s actual shipping history. By examining these records, importers can tell whether a company is a real factory or a middleman trading outfit, turning supplier vetting into an evidence-based process rather than a trust exercise.
